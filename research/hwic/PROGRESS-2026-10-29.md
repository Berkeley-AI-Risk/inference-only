# HWIC research progress — October 29, 2026

The [October 27 SimpleStories source checkpoint](simplestories-2026-10-27/README.md)
remains the published executable baseline. Subsequent local work has advanced
component optimizations and validation tooling. This progress note adds no
replacement RTL or new public simulation package. A complete qualified ASIC and
a verified ASIC token rate remain open.

The research direction draws on Yang Liu et al., [*Hardwired-Neurons Language
Processing Units as General-Purpose Cognitive Substrates*,
arXiv:2508.16151v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16151v2). Our baseline uses fixed ROM
feeding shared arithmetic and adapts the AWIC token core. HNLPU's custom physical
weight-routing implementation has not been reproduced; no comparative area or
speed advantage has been established.

These recent source-review records remain local. This note summarizes their
scope rather than packaging independently reproducible public proofs. Source
review is distinct from running the hardware simulation, and existing failures
remain recorded.

## Reuse and research priorities

A byte comparison finds 39 of the published HWIC baseline's 67 synthesizable
SystemVerilog files identical to the packaged TangMega AWIC. The newer candidate
retains those same 39 matches among 1,961 synthesizable files. These are source
file counts, not percentages of chip area, gates or elaborated instances.

SimpleStories remains the first ASIC target, using W10/A16/KV16 BFP v5.
Qwen3-1.7B remains a secondary target with separate 8-bit research; Qwen3-8B HWIC
work is deferred while its AWIC improves. Reusable components and spatial
parallelism remain priorities. Batching will be evaluated separately.

## Remaining milestones

1. Complete the pending execution-tool validation and run the fresh mapped-ROM
   pipeline with its numerical checks and retained prior evidence.
2. Complete the newer integrated full-model positive and fault regression before
   promoting its RTL to the published baseline.
3. Qualify physical ROM/SRAM and resolve the remaining memory geometry and LVS
   issues. Complete whole-chip placement, routing, extraction and DRC/LVS,
   together with clock/reset and manufacturing-test design.
4. Evaluate PVT timing, power and whole-chip area, then report single-conversation
   throughput against a matched GPU workload.

No qualified clock frequency, silicon measurement or manufacturing readiness is
claimed. The current simulation results and source checks do not establish those
properties.
